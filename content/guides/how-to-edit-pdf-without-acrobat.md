Adobe Acrobat is the most well-known PDF editor, but at $19.99/month it's overkill for most people. Here's how to edit a PDF without it — and what "editing" actually means depending on what you need to do.

## First: what kind of editing do you actually need?

"Edit a PDF" means very different things depending on the situation:

- **Fill in a form** — type into existing form fields or blank lines
- **Add text or annotations** — add comments, sticky notes, or text boxes on top
- **Change existing text** — actually modify words that are already in the document
- **Add or remove pages** — reorganize the document structure
- **Add images or signatures** — insert visual elements

Each of these has different tool requirements. True text editing (changing existing words in the document) is the hardest — and most people don't actually need it as often as they think.

## For annotating and commenting

**Any PDF viewer** handles this:
- **Preview (Mac)** — highlight, add sticky notes, draw, add text boxes, sign
- **Adobe Acrobat Reader (free)** — comment, highlight, and annotate without a subscription
- **Chrome/Edge** — basic highlighting and notes built into the browser PDF viewer

## For changing existing text in the document

This is the hardest task and where free tools fall short. Your options:

**Option 1: Go back to the source.** If the PDF was created from a Word doc, Google Doc, or InDesign file, edit the original and re-export. This always gives the cleanest result.

**Option 2: Convert to Word, edit, convert back.** Use a PDF-to-Word converter, make your changes in Word, export back to PDF. Works well for simple documents; complex layouts need manual cleanup.

**Option 3: PDFelement.** The best paid alternative to Acrobat for direct text editing, at around $79/year — significantly cheaper than Acrobat.
